1Spending buys years, but only up to a point

Across the world, countries that spend more on their health tend to live longer. The question is whether that point holds at every level of spending, or whether the gains level off for the countries that spend the most.

Overall, higher spending comes with longer lives up to about $2,000 per person. Above that most countries live 80-85 years no matter what they spend. Some countries even live less than their spending predicts, like the US with -6.5 years less than expected. However, a single year cannot show how countries got there, or how well they managed when Covid-19 hit.

2Ireland got more for its money than the US

Over the years, Ireland went from below the UK and the US to above both, while spending less than half as much as the US, and lost less life expectancy during the pandemic. However, life expectancy alone cannot tell us whether those extra years were lived in good health. These last two charts do not show spending, but test the "healthier" half of the question.

3Ireland's extra years were healthy ones

So, does spending more on healthcare buy a longer, healthier life? Up to about $2,000 per person, strongly. Above that, barely. Ireland lives longer than the US on less than half the spending, and up to 2019 its healthy years grew even faster than its life expectancy.
